Cross-references

The annotated layer of the atlas: links the author chose and explained. The app also draws a wider web of verse pairs from the dataset’s reference listing and from verse-aligned quotation blocks.

Quotation (27)

Isaiah 2–14 → 2 Nephi 12–24weight 5

Thirteen consecutive chapters of Isaiah copied onto Nephi's plates — the largest single quotation in the canon. Nephi frames it: "that I might more fully persuade them to believe in the Lord their Redeemer" (1 Ne 19:23).

Isaiah 48–49 → 1 Nephi 20–21weight 4

Nephi reads the servant-and-scattered-Israel chapters to his brothers aboard-ship distance from Jerusalem — then "likens" them: scattered Israel includes his own family.

Isaiah 50–51 → 2 Nephi 7–8weight 3

Jacob₂ preaches the covenant-comfort chapters at Nephi's request: "awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of the Lord."

Isaiah 53 → Mosiah 14weight 4

Abinadi recites the suffering-servant chapter entire, in chains, as the centerpiece of his defense — then expounds it verse by verse (Mosiah 15).

Isaiah 52:7–10 → Mosiah 12:21–24weight 2

Noah's priests weaponize "how beautiful upon the mountains" as a gotcha question; Abinadi's whole trial-sermon is his answer.

Isaiah 54 → 3 Nephi 22weight 3

The risen Christ quotes the barren-woman covenant chapter to the Nephites, then commands: "great are the words of Isaiah… search these things diligently."

Isaiah 29 → 2 Nephi 26–27weight 3

Nephi reworks the sealed-book prophecy into an extended prophecy of the Book of Mormon itself — "the words of a book that is sealed."

Isaiah 11 → 2 Nephi 30:9–15weight 2

The wolf-and-lamb millennium re-quoted in Nephi's closing prophecy of restoration.

Malachi 3–4 → 3 Nephi 24–25weight 3

The risen Christ dictates two chapters the Nephites could not have carried with them — Malachi wrote after Lehi left — and explains why: "it was expedient that they should be given unto future generations."

Deuteronomy 18:15–19 → 3 Nephi 20:23weight 2

The risen Christ applies Moses' "prophet like unto me" to himself — the same proof-text Peter uses in Acts 3.

Micah 5:8–15 → 3 Nephi 21:12–21weight 2

The remnant-as-lion prophecy re-voiced by Christ concerning the seed of Lehi among the Gentiles.

Deuteronomy 6–8 → Matthew 4:1–11weight 2

All three of Jesus' answers to the tempter come from Moses' wilderness sermon — the forty days recapitulate the forty years.

Isaiah 61:1–2 → Luke 4:16–21weight 3

The Nazareth synagogue reading — "this day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears" — is Jesus' own mission statement, in Isaiah's words.

Isaiah 53:7–8 → Acts 8:26–35weight 2

The Ethiopian eunuch's chariot reading: "of whom speaketh the prophet this?" Philip "began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus."

Psalms 110 → Hebrews 5–7weight 3

"A priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec" — the psalm on which Hebrews builds its whole high-priest christology.

Daniel 9:27 → Matthew 24:15–16weight 1

The Olivet discourse cites "the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet."

Psalms 118:22–26 → Matthew 21:9–42weight 2

Hosanna, "blessed is he that cometh," and the rejected stone — one psalm supplies both the triumphal entry and its parable of judgment.

Jeremiah 31:31–34 → Hebrews 8:8–13weight 2

The new-covenant oracle quoted at length — the longest OT citation in the NT.

Matthew 5–7 → 3 Nephi 12–14weight 5

The Sermon at the Temple: the Sermon on the Mount redelivered at Bountiful with covenantal reframings ("come unto me" for "the kingdom of heaven") — the canon's largest NT–BoM parallel.

Acts 3:22–26 → 3 Nephi 20:23–26weight 2

Peter and the risen Christ deploy the same Deuteronomy 18 proof-text, in nearly identical wording, to audiences an ocean apart.

Genesis 1–6 → Moses 2–8weight 5

The book of Moses runs parallel to the opening of Genesis — the same narrative retold with a cosmic prologue and substantial expansions, framed as revelation to Moses "in a day when the mountains were before him."

Matthew 24 → Joseph Smith--Matthew 1weight 4

The Olivet discourse re-given: Matthew 24 with reordered sequence and clarifying expansions — the Pearl of Great Price's direct engagement with the Gospel text.

James 1:5 → Joseph Smith--History 1:11–13weight 2

"If any of you lack wisdom…" — quoted in the history as the verse that "did enter with more power into the heart" than any other, and sent a fourteen-year-old to the grove.

Malachi 3–4 → Joseph Smith--History 1:36–39weight 2

Moroni quotes Malachi's closing chapters "with a little variation" — the same chapters the risen Christ dictated at Bountiful, now framing the Restoration.

Isaiah 11 → Joseph Smith--History 1:40weight 1

The stem-of-Jesse chapter cited by Moroni as "about to be fulfilled."

Joel 2:28–32 → Joseph Smith--History 1:41weight 1

Peter's Pentecost text quoted a second time — Moroni adds that it "was not yet fulfilled, but was soon to be."

Isaiah 6:9–10 → John 12:37–41weight 1

John cites the temple-vision hardening oracle and adds: "these things said Esaias, when he saw his glory."

Allusion (17)

Isaiah 55:1–2 → 2 Nephi 9:50–51weight 1

"Ho, every one that thirsteth" — Jacob₂ folds Isaiah's invitation into his atonement discourse.

Genesis 50:24–26 → 2 Nephi 3weight 3

Lehi blesses his last son with prophecies of Joseph of Egypt concerning a future seer — an extended midrash on the Joseph narrative, applied to Lehi's own line.

Genesis 11:1–9 → Ether 1:33–38weight 2

The book of Ether opens at "the great tower" of Babel — the Jaredite migration is a road out of Genesis 11.

Exodus 14 → 1 Nephi 17:23–44weight 3

Nephi's ship-building apologia retells the Exodus at length: his family's sea crossing is a deliberate second Red Sea.

1 Corinthians 13 → Moroni 7:44–48weight 3

Mormon's charity sermon converges with Paul's hymn nearly phrase for phrase — "charity suffereth long, and is kind" — then defines it: "the pure love of Christ."

1 Corinthians 12:4–11 → Moroni 10:8–17weight 2

Moroni's catalogue of spiritual gifts tracks Paul's list — the book's final chapter speaks Corinthian.

Hebrews 11 → Ether 12:6–22weight 3

Two catalogues of faith: "faith is things hoped for and not seen" — Moroni's roll-call of Book of Mormon believers answers Hebrews' roll-call of Hebrew ones.

John 21:20–23 → 3 Nephi 28:1–9weight 2

The tarrying grant given to John is given again to three Nephite disciples — the two texts explicitly cross-reference each other.

James 1:5 → Moroni 10:4–5weight 2

Two invitations to ask God directly — the epistle's "if any of you lack wisdom" and Moroni's promise — historically paired as the Book of Mormon's front and back doors.

Revelation 1:1–3 → 1 Nephi 14:18–27weight 2

Nephi is shown the apostle of the future Apocalypse and told the coming book is his to write: "the name of the apostle of the Lamb was John."

Romans 11:16–24 → Jacob 5weight 3

Paul's grafted olive branches in eleven verses; Zenos' in seventy-seven — the same horticultural theology of scattered Israel at two very different scales.

Matthew 26:26–28 → 3 Nephi 18:1–11weight 2

The sacrament instituted twice: bread and wine at Jerusalem's last supper, and again — with the multitude fed — at Bountiful.

John 17 → 3 Nephi 19:19–29weight 2

A second intercessory prayer, this time with the praying Christ visible: "Father, I thank thee that thou hast purified those whom I have chosen."

Genesis 5:18–24 → Moses 6–7weight 3

Five verses of Genesis Enoch become two chapters: the preaching, the city of Zion taken up, and the vision of the weeping God — the canon's largest single expansion of a biblical figure.

Genesis 11–17 → Abraham 1–2weight 3

Ur, Haran, Egypt, and the covenant retold in Abraham's own first person — with an altar-rescue prologue and an astronomy lesson Genesis never gives.

Genesis 1:1–3 → John 1:1–5weight 2

"In the beginning" answered with "In the beginning was the Word" — John rewrites the creation overture around the Logos.

Exodus 3:14 → John 8:56–58weight 2

"Before Abraham was, I am" — the bush-name claimed aloud; the stones picked up immediately after show the audience understood.

Fulfillment (11)

Isaiah 7:14 → Matthew 1:22–23weight 2

"Behold, a virgin shall conceive" — Matthew's first and programmatic fulfillment citation.

Micah 5:2 → Matthew 2:5–6weight 2

The chief priests answer Herod from Micah: Bethlehem, "little among the thousands of Judah."

Isaiah 40:3 → Matthew 3:1–3weight 2

"The voice of one crying in the wilderness" — all four Gospels introduce the Baptist with this verse.

Psalms 22 → Matthew 27:35–46weight 3

Parted garments, wagging heads, and the cry "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me" — the crucifixion narrated through the psalm.

Joel 2:28–32 → Acts 2:16–21weight 2

Peter's Pentecost text: "your sons and your daughters shall prophesy."

Malachi 3:1 → Mark 1:2weight 1

"Behold, I send my messenger" — Mark opens his Gospel with Malachi's courier.

Zechariah 12:10 → John 19:34–37weight 1

"They shall look upon me whom they have pierced" — John's eyewitness note at the spear-thrust.

Hosea 11:1 → Matthew 2:14–15weight 1

"Out of Egypt have I called my son" — Israel's exodus re-read as the child's.

John 10:16 → 3 Nephi 15:16–24weight 3

"Other sheep I have, which are not of this fold" — the risen Christ tells the Nephites they are the sentence's referent, unbeknownst to Jerusalem.

Matthew 27:45–54 → 3 Nephi 8:5–23weight 3

The crucifixion darkness, hemispheres apart: three hours in Judea, three days in the New World — with earthquake, rent rocks, and voices in the dark.

Malachi 4:5–6 → Luke 1:16–17weight 1

Gabriel frames the Baptist's mission in Malachi's Elijah oracle: "to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children."

Typology (8)

Genesis 37 → Alma 46:23–27weight 2

Moroni₁ reads his rent coat against Joseph's rent coat: "let us remember the words of Jacob" — the title of liberty gains a patriarchal genealogy.

Numbers 21:6–9 → Helaman 8:14–15weight 2

Nephi₂ reads the brass serpent exactly as John 3 does: "as he lifted up the brazen serpent… even so shall he be lifted up." A triple link across all three volumes.

Genesis 22:1–14 → Hebrews 11:17–19weight 2

The binding of Isaac read as resurrection-parable: Abraham "accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead."

Jonah 1:17 → Matthew 12:38–41weight 2

The only sign given "this evil and adulterous generation": three days in the fish, three days in the earth.

Numbers 21:8–9 → John 3:14–15weight 2

"As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up" — the same typology Nephite prophets draw independently.

Genesis 3 → Romans 5:12–19weight 2

Adam as "the figure of him that was to come": Paul's first-and-second-Adam architecture — which 2 Nephi 2 builds independently from the brass plates.

Exodus 12 → 1 Corinthians 5:7weight 1

"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us" — the feast made christology in one clause.

Psalms 23 → John 10:11–18weight 1

The Lord-as-shepherd psalm behind "I am the good shepherd."
